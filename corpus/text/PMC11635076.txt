# Gold nanoparticle encapsulated hybrid MOF: synthesis, characterization, and co-drug delivery of 5-fluorouracil and curcumin
## Abstract
AbstractThe unique features of Metal–Organic Frameworks (MOFs), including structural flexibility, high surface area, and variable pore size, have drawn attention in cancer therapy. However, despite advances in surface functionalization, engineering structural features, and porosity, achieving controlled release, stability, scalability, and toxicity remains a challenge. The current study reports gold nanoparticle (AuNP) encapsulated dual metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) comprising zeolitic imidazolate (ZIF8) and cobalt-imidazole (ZIF67) by a simple precipitation method for dual drug delivery applications. This combination associates the advantages of AuNPs and MOFs, creating a potent platform for cancer theranostics that combines diagnosis and treatment into one unit. The synthesized composite (AuNPs@ZIF-8/ZIF-67) is functionalized with Folic acid (FA) and loaded with the anticancer agents Curcumin (C) and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) for co-drug delivery The synthesized composites, namely Au/ZIF8, Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA, Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA/5-FU, and Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA/5-FU/C were characterized using diverse analytical techniques such as FESEM, XRD, FTIR, TEM, and BET. The characterization methods showed that the hybrid MOF structure was stable and intact after AuNP encapsulation and drug loading. The dual MOF composite exhibits a better affinity for loading C and 5-FU with 60% and 40% drug loading capacity, respectively. The simultaneous drug release studies suggest that AuNPs@ZIF-8/ZIF-67 are more responsive to the acidic pH and show a higher cumulative drug release of 5FU and C at the lower value of pH 5. For further validation, the release kinetics data were fitted into the Korsmeyer-Peppas model in the current study. The observed value of n which is less than 0.5 suggests the pseudo-Fickian diffusion mechanism for drug release, demonstrating long-term release of 5FU and C from Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA/5-FU/C. The targeted drug delivery system is anticipated to display synergistic therapeutic efficacy from the combined effect of the two anticancer agents and the pH-responsive nature of ZIF systems.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s11671-024-04152-z.Keywords: Cancer, Drug delivery, Curcumin, 5-Fluorouracil, Dual MOF, ZIF-8/ZIF-67
## Introduction
Metal–organic frameworks (MOFs), which comprise various metal ions as nodes and organic linkers, have sparked widespread interest in the research field because of exciting properties such as permanent ultra-high porosity, a large surface area, a tunable shape, and versatile functionality [, ]. MOF properties include excellent biodegradability, low cytotoxicity, and versatile functionality, making them ideal candidates as drug delivery hosts []. MOF materials’ structure relies on the coordination bond between the organic linkers and inorganic metal ions or clusters of metal ions []. Compared to the traditional reported porous materials like zeolites [], porous carbons [], and mesoporous silica [, ], MOFs have additional characteristics like high surface area, ordered porous structure, functional pore space and are promising nanocarriers in different biomedical applications []. The properties and applications of dual MOF-based composites mainly depend on the intrinsic structure and morphology of MOF composites. Therefore, combining nanoparticle (NP) and dual MOFs as a composite can exhibit new properties that one discrete constituent may not have [, ]. Different NPs, ranging from several nanometers with excellent chemical activity and surface area, suffer from intrinsic instability [, ]. Encapsulating such NP inside porous materials like MOFs can subside drawbacks, and NP/MOF nanocomposite can be explored for different biomedical applications [, ]. Enhanced biodegradability, biocompatibility, and MOFs’ ability to rapidly degrade in biological settings and facilitate the release of drugs due to the weak interactions between their coordinative bonds []. Stiff organic ligands with stiff backbone networks help preserve the open pore structure and facilitate the prediction of a molecule’s type and configuration. The continuous porosity behavior of MOF has greatly facilitated host–guest interactions and enabled the adsorption of guest molecules. For instance, MOFs have drawn much attention in applications related to imaging and therapy [].
Surface engineering or the surface functionalization of these MOFs is significant for its ability to target the specific disease site []. The MOFs’ surface can be upgraded with diverse functional groups, ligands, and antigens to target specific sites []. The drug molecules might be enclosed inside the MOF core or adsorbed onto the MOF’s surface [, ]. Anticancer drug 5-fluoro-1H-pyrimidine-2,4-dione, commonly known as 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) is mainly used to treat colorectal cancer []. 5-FU acts effectively; it inhibits essential biosynthetic processes or is incorporated into DNA and RNA, inhibiting normal function [, ]. (1E,6E)-1,7-bis(4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)hepta-1,6-diene-3,5-dione generally known as curcumin (C), effectively works as an anticancer agent by suppressing cellular signaling pathways, which induces apoptosis and inhibits proliferation [, ].
To accomplish effective NP-based drug delivery, it is necessary to understand some key components, including the interactions of NPs with the biological environment, drug release mechanisms, drug administration routes, molecular mechanisms of cell signaling, and the stability of therapeutic agents. [, ]. Utilizing NPs also allows most of them to be absorbed by cells more quickly than giant macromolecules, facilitating more effective drug delivery and transportation [–]. A drug delivery system that can generate a prolonged therapeutic impact by releasing the therapeutic material gradually over a protracted period, usually days or months, after a single dose is administered, is known as the sustained drug release delivery system. This kind of delivery method usually keeps the body’s therapeutic drug levels at optimal levels for a longer amount of time, which lowers the need for frequent doses, minimizes side effects, maintains steady levels of drug uptake in blood and plasma, and increases patient compliance [].
Zeolitic imidazolate frameworks (ZIFs) are perfect for biomedical applications because of their large pores, stability in physiological conditions, and low toxicity []. A recent study demonstrated that Zn2⁺ in ZIF8 correlates with an increase in ROS generation, which causes a cell cycle arrest in the G2/M phase and, eventually, activates apoptosis []. The therapeutic advantages of the ZIF8 include substantial colloidal dispersibility, high drug loading capacity, pH-sensitive degradation, and high thermal/chemical stability []. MOF-on-MOF composites have emerged as a prominent area of research recently []. They are created by assembling two or more homogeneous or heterogeneous MOFs with distinct morphologies and structures []. Compared to single MOFs, dual MOF-on-MOF composites have added tunability, high surface area, and improved performance in biomedical applications []. Using the epitaxial growth method, MOFs with similar cell parameters can create MOF-on-MOF composites. This is one of the most popular techniques, and it can produce composites with varying content and structure []. In this approach, a host MOF is first synthesized as the substrate. The guest MOF with similar cell parameters is then epitaxially grown on the interface, producing MOF on MOF nanocomposite with well-matched facet orientation and lattice distance with host MOF []. ZIF8/ZIF67 MOF-on-MOF hybrids have been synthesized successfully using this method []. Gao et al. created a drug-loaded, targeted imaging agent (ZIF-8/5-FU@FA-CHI-5-FAM) by loading ZIF8 MOF with 5-FU and coating with a 5-carboxyfluorescein (5-FAM) and FA functionalized polymer []. Encapsulating NPs inside cavities in similar targeted MOFs can impart properties to the composites that are advantageous for cancer treatment. Considering various NPs available for preparing MOF composites, AuNPs possess low toxicity, a high degree of synthetic control, and size tenability []. All these properties help to design versatile composite structures, particularly for bio applications [, ]. Numerous biomedical applications have recently been attempted for AuNPs encapsulated within MOF, and synthesis strategies have been well-established previously [].
The size, shape, and surface chemistry of AuNPs influence their physical characteristics, which will, in turn, impact their ability to transport anticancer agents. Therefore, before building carrier molecules, it is crucial to assess the characteristics of AuNPs carefully []. Additionally, AuNPs are produced as viable drug carriers capable of binding various drugs and specific ligands. With AuNPs, surface modification allows sustainable and controlled delivery of anticancer agents []. ZIF8 and ZIF67 were employed in those studies as the host framework for Au NPs loaded with a diameter of around 10 nm. Numerous studies show how solvent parity affects where Au NPs are located concerning MOFs []. However, the primary goal of adding Au NPs to MOFs is to improve their biomedical capabilities. Ideally, Au NPs would be regularly inserted into each ZIF8 and ZIF67 pore. Here, we present an easy way to create Au@ZIF8/ZIF67, or Au NPs integrated into ZIF8, systematically []. The therapeutic characteristics of the resultant material were then examined by studying the drug release kinetics.
The current study reports, AuNP encapsulated inside dual MOF (ZIF8/ZIF67) by a simple precipitation method. The synthesized composite is loaded with the anticancer agent’s C and 5-FU for co-delivery of anticancer agents. Additionally, we have emphasized the use of stimulus-responsive nano-MOFs as dual drug delivery systems, which present a novel approach to the creation of hybrid nanocarriers that may overcome the limitations of pure organic and inorganic carriers, including toxicity, burst effect, and reduced payload capacity. The problem addressed in this study is that selecting drug combinations that lower the risk of chemotherapy resistance in cancer patients can improve the synergistic effect of combination therapy. Another possibility for creating sophisticated drug delivery systems that react to specific internal and external stimuli is provided by nano-based formulations.
In the present study, AuNPs@ZIF-8/ZIF-67 (dual MOF) composite particles were synthesized, functionalized with folic acid, and loaded with 5-FU and C. One of the extensively researched methods for focusing on cancer cells is targeting the folate receptor. Since healthy living cells have a higher pH than cancer cells, the ZIF system’s pH responsiveness will ensure that the anticancer drugs are released selectively. The synthesized systems’ susceptibility is evaluated by analyzing drug release characteristics at varying pH values. Combining the two anticancer drugs and the pH-responsiveness of ZIF systems should result in an enhanced therapeutic efficacy for the synthesized targeted drug delivery system. ## Materials and methods
## Materials
All chemicals were of the analytical grade and utilized directly after purchase without further purification. 2-methylimidazole (2-MeIM, Sigma Aldrich, India), zinc nitrate hexahydrate (Zn (NO3)2·6H2O, Sigma-Aldrich, India), cobalt nitrate hexahydrate (Co (NO3)2·6H2O, Sigma-Aldrich, India), 5-flurouracil (≥ 99% (HPLC) (Sigma Aldrich, India), Folic acid (Sigma Aldrich, India), N, N Dimethylformamide (DMF, Sigma Aldrich, India), Curcumin (≥ 94% (HPLC)) (Sigma Aldrich, India), trisodium citrate (SD fine chemicals, India), tetrachloroauric acid (Sigma Aldrich, India), Ethanol, and Methanol were received from Avra Synthesis Pvt Ltd, India.
## Methods
## Gold nanoparticles synthesis (AuNP)
Considering a few modifications, conventional citrate reduction methodology was used to synthesize gold nanoparticles []. Initially, 100 mL of 1 mM tetrachloroauric acid (HAuCl4) solution is refluxed (90℃) for 20 min. 10 mL of 38.8 mM trisodium citrate (fresh) is added dropwise to the tetrachloroauric acid (HAuCl4) solution and further heated for 30 min at the same temperature. After observing a change in the colour of the solution, it is agitated for a further 30 min at room temperature. While the solution reaches room temperature, the pH is adjusted to 7 using 0.1 M NaOH. The AuNP is washed by centrifugation at 1200 rpm with double distilled water. The concentration of AuNP solution is determined from the UV–visible absorption spectrum at 520 nm using the Beer-Lambert law []. The stock concentration of the gold nanoparticles used is 1 nM.
## AuNP-encapsulated ZIF8 (Au/ZIF8)
To synthesize AuNP-encapsulated ZIF8, a bottle-around-ship synthesis strategy is followed []. The pre-synthesized, centrifuged AuNP solution (100 ul, 1 nM) is added to a 10 mL 2-MeIM (12 mM) solution and ultrasonicated for 10 min. This solution is added to a 30 mL, Zn (NO3)2·6H2O (3 mM) solution under continuous stirring and stirred further for 24 h (400 rpm). Centrifugation at 4000 rpm (10 min) is used to extract the product once the reaction is finished. Finally, the product is washed with methanol and dried at 45 °C for 3 h and designated as Au/ZIF8.
## Synthesis of AuNP@ZIF8@ZIF67@FA (Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA)
The following procedure is followed to make the targeted composite with FA functionalization to dual MOF structure []. Pre-synthesized Au/ZIF8 (55 mg) is dispersed in 15 mL methanol and sonicated for 15 min (solution A). The second MOF cage is derived from Co(NO3)2·6H2O and 2-MeIM precursors. Co(NO3)26H2O, (2 g) is dissolved in solution A and stirred for 30 min at 350 rpm. Then, 0.015 g of folic acid (FA), dissolved in 5 mL DMF, is added to solution A. Solution B is prepared by dissolving 0.50 g of 2-MeIM in methanol (10 mL). In the final step, solution B is added dropwise to solution A and stirred at 800 rpm for 30 min. The yellow-colored final material separated once stirring was stopped. The product is centrifuged, rinsed with methanol multiple times (10 min at 3000 rpm), and dried for 12 h (50 °C). The synthesized nanocomposite is designated as Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA.
## 5-FU and C drug loading and release studies (Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA/5-FU/C)
Anticancer agents C and 5-FU are incorporated on Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA hybrid material by properly mixing with methanol and drug (1 mg/mL) in a shaker for prolonged time. The prepared 40 mg of Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA composite is dispersed in 40 mL of methanol solution of C and 5-FU. The above mixture is kept in a shaker for 12 h. Finally, the solution was centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 15 min, and the drug-loaded composite materials were washed twice with methanol, followed by deionized water. Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA/5-FU/C is freeze-dried at -55 °C for 24 h using a lyophilizer (ALPHA 1-2LDplus, CHRIST). The obtained supernatant is collected to determine the drug loading. The drug loading capacity (DLC) was calculated using Eq. (1). The drug loading efficiency (DLE) was calculated using Eq. (2).
For drug release studies, 40 mg of Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA/5-FU/C is dispersed in 40 mL of PBS solution of desired pH (7.4, 6.2, and 5) and kept in a shaker at 37 °C. The solution is centrifuged at 10000 rpm for about 2 min, and 500 µl supernatant is collected. The amount of drug released is measured with a UV–visible spectrophotometer. The sample collection was done at specific time intervals: 30 min, 1 h, 1 h.30 min, 2 h, 2 h.30 min, 3 h, 3 h.30 min, 4 h, 6 h, 8 h, 12 h, 24 h, 28 h, 32 h, 48 h, 60 h etc. UV–Vis spectrophotometer was used to monitor the absorption bands of the anticancer agents at wavelength 425 nm and 276 nm for C and 5-FU, respectively. The corresponding volume of fresh buffer solutions is added into the drug release media to keep pH constant during the measurements.
## Characterization
The particle morphology of the nanocomposites is analyzed using a Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM), Thermo Fisher Titan Themis- 300 kV, FEG source, Japan. A field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM) in conjunction with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX), JEOL, JSM, and 7100-F, Germany are used to analyze surface morphology and elemental analysis. The surface area and pore volume are assessed using a Belsorp-Max surface area analyzer, Microtrac BEL, Japan. Powder X-ray diffraction patterns were recorded at 40 kV and 30 mA using Ni-filtered Cu Kα radiation (l = 1.5406 A°) at a 2θ scan speed of 1.0° min−1 in the 5–80° range. The data was collected using an Ultima IV X-ray diffractometer (Rigaku Corporation, Japan). Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra of all the composites were recorded using the KBr pellet method on the Bruker FTIR Spectrometer ALPHA-E Germany. UV–visible spectra to follow drug loading and release were recorded on a Shimadzu UV-1800, A11454907691 Germany.
## Results and discussion
In therapeutic applications, simultaneous co-drug delivery via MOFs has numerous important benefits. MOFs can improve treatment outcomes by delivering numerous drugs simultaneously and creating synergistic effects. MOFs improve patient compliance and lessen side effects by enabling precise, regulated release profiles for both drugs. Encapsulation within MOFs can increase the stability and bioavailability of drugs by preventing degradation. Inspired by this approach, we developed a nanocomposite system called Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA, made up of therapeutic molecules 5FU and C and Au nanoparticles enclosed in a dual MOF composite tethered to FA as a targeting agent. Scheme 2 provides a comprehensive design and synthesis method of Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA/5-FU/C. The MOF-AuNP system can be used as a diagnostic and therapeutic platform. By combining the benefits of MOFs with AuNPs, this combination creates a powerful platform for cancer theranostics that integrates diagnostics and treatment into a single unit. Dual MOFs were tagged with FA for specific targeting. This led to the synthesis of Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA. Then, to attain a high drug loading capacity, the 5FU and C drugs were loaded on FA-tagged dual MOF.
## Characterization of nanocomposites:
FESEM (Fig. 1) analysis examines the synthesized nanocomposite structure and morphology (Au/Z8, Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA, Au/ZIF8ZIF67/FA/5FU-C). Au-embedded ZIF8 nanocomposites are monodisperse and show uniform polyhedron morphology with a size range of 600 ± 10 nm (Fig. 1a, b). The epitaxial growth of ZIF67 and loading of FA on pre-synthesized Au/ZIF8 nanoparticles formed a core–shell polyhedron structure. Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA shows no significant morphological changes, but a size increase can be observed (50 ± 10 nm) (Fig. 1c, d). After loading the drug 5-FU, as shown in Fig. 1e, f, the bulk morphology of the nanoparticle was retained, with more agglomerated petal-like structures on the surface. After loading with the dual anticancer agents 5-FU and C, the nanocomposite particles have a more pronounced flower-like agglomerated structure (Fig. 1g, h). Agglomeration behaviors in nanomaterials can be classified by two different types: dry and wet. In wet agglomeration, due to the volatile solvent, the powder particles are bound to aggregates, and then separated by drying, though cohesion forces are not appropriate to support the aggregates. Wet agglomeration involves three main steps: (i) wetting and nucleation, (ii) coalescence and consolidation, and (iii) breakage and attrition. The MOF agglomeration is frequently achieved by the centrifugal agglomeration []. In contrast, these processes permit the growth of aggregates in various particle densities and sizes []. The observed findings are in good agreement with the reported literature as reported by Kamatchirajan et al. []. They observed when the gold is electrochemically deposited, the uniform hexagon-shaped ZIF-8 particles are completely transferred into an aggregated structure, allowing it to be verified that gold was deposited as gold nanoparticles both within and outside of the aggregated ZIF-8.
Since MOFs can be incorporated into composites like anticancer agents and various biomolecules to increase their biocompatibility and potential medical applications, they are primarily used as drug delivery vehicles rather than therapeutic agents []. Nanoparticles larger than 500 nm are primarily internalized at the injection site []. High porosity and high surface area to volume ratio allow high levels of substitution, making MOFs ideal for drug delivery.

Interestingly the primary particle structure remains the same in all stages. The image of composite Au/ZIF8 (Fig. 2 a, b) shows a polyhedron structure. The formation of petal-like extensions on the particle surface can be seen from the addition of the second MOF structure and the loading of the 5-FU and C anticancer agents (Fig. 2c, d, e, f). Regulating the structure, physicochemical properties, and particle size of dominant MOFs remains challenging. However, their capacity to encapsulate large-sized pharmaceuticals enables enhanced encapsulation efficiency and improved drug loading []. Furthermore, modifying the MOF structure may encourage a greater loading of anticancer agents level via enhanced host drug interactions (van der Waals or hydrogen bonding) between drugs and MOFs []. The N–H and C-H stretching vibrations of the methyl and terminal amine groups in ZIF-8’s imidazole rings correspond to the strong absorption bands in the 2900–3200 cm−1 range. Stretching vibrations at 1628 cm−1 arise from the stretching vibrations of alkenes (C = C) and carbonyl (C = O) in the C drug (Fig. 5b).

MOFs are characterized by their large surface area and structural deviation, which facilitate the loading of anticancer agents using different loading techniques. To maintain particle structure, drug-loading methods for loading anticancer agents into the framework of MOFs are thoroughly investigated []. Since the molecular dimensions of anticancer agents are smaller than the pore size of MOFs, they are generally expected to be entrapped inside the scaffolds by hydrogen bonding interaction, or other host–guest interaction. Thus, MOFs are projected to harness electrostatic attraction to adsorb larger molecules of anticancer drugs with opposing charges []. Due to these interactions, MOF’s structure, morphology, and pore size are expected to alter post-drug loading.
## Co-drug delivery studies
Improved treatment efficacy, targeted distribution, and controlled release of therapeutic agents are anticipated outcomes of the combination therapy employing drug delivery systems. It is still difficult to produce single drug carriers that can administer hydrophilic 5-FU and hydrophobic C, both drugs, at the same time []. When 5-FU is taken in combination with drugs such as metformin, doxorubicin, paclitaxel, and curcumin, therapeutic efficacy and response rates are increased in cancer therapy []. However, Curcumin’s limited stability and solubility make it challenging and emphasize the need for innovative co-delivery methods to ensure the regulated release and maximize therapeutic potential. Measuring the equilibrium solubility of C and 5FU might provide a better strategy, but the drug release media and volume selection relied on prior research to maintain conditions [, ]. Surfactants may increase curcumin solubility, although the parameters selected were meant to resemble conditions in which surfactants are not used. The objective was to evaluate both drugs for co-delivery systems under uniform conditions, even though independent profiling studies would offer different insights.
In the present study, ZIF8/ZIF67 dual MOF composite confirms the substantial drug loading capacity for 5FU and C 40 and 60% in composite AuNPs@ZIF-8/ZIF-67, respectively. The drug loading efficiency was found to be 96 and 97% for 5FU and C, respectively. The specific time-dependent release systems are designed to deliver drugs at predefined time points. An initial burst release occurs, usually by releasing anti-cancer agents bound close to the surface and is followed by a sustained release rate [, ]. The in-vitro drug release profiles for 5-FU and C from Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA/5-FU/C are shown in Fig. 7a–c. The drug release was analyzed for 60 h. Drug release was studied in PBS at pH 5 (Fig. 7a), 6.2 (Fig. 7b), and 7.4 (Fig. 7c). pH 5 is similar to the more acidic microenvironment of tumors []. Hence, an anticancer drug delivery system is more advantageous when drug release profiles are responsive to pH around pH 5. A total drug release of 29% is observed within a period of 8 h. Compared to the release profiles obtained at higher pH, the release at pH 5 plateaus to a higher value within the same period (~ 10 h). The plaeuted value at pH 5 is much more advanced than that observed at basic pH but closer to the value obtained at lower acidity. C shows a lower release compared to 5-FU. The release pattern is comparable for both anticancer agents, indicating a weak interaction between the anticancer agents and the career system. Another effective strategy is to use the features of the tumor’s microenvironment, such as the malignant environment’s lower pH (which varies from 5 to 7) compared to the pH in normal tissues (7.35–7.45). This means that developing pH-dependent delivery systems that release material reacting to an acidic pH level opens up new possibilities for treating challenging tumors [, ]. Human bodies frequently have a pH that is strictly in biological equilibrium. However, the pH in connective tissue might differ from that in blood plasma, depending on environmental circumstances and cell metabolism. This makes it possible to administer drugs targeted using pH-sensitive processes [].
We explored drug release kinetics to comprehend the release behaviors of both drugs. To gain insight into the kinetics and mechanism of drug release, an investigation was conducted on the cumulative drug release data of 5FU and C. Our group observed that the MOF-based drug carriers exhibit pseudo-Fickian behavior as described in our recent report on iron oxide encapsulated ZIF8/ZIF67 nanocomposite for quercetin release [, ]. Hence, the release kinetics data were fitted into the Korsmeyer-Peppas model for additional validation in the present investigation. Figure S3 and Figure S4 in the Supplementary Information exhibit the detailed analysis. The value of n was less than 0.5 following fitting the release kinetics data in the Korsmeyer-Peppas model, showing a pseudo-Fickian diffusion mechanism indicating the diffusion-controlled, slow, and long-term drug release of 5FU and C from Au/ZIF8/ZIF67/FA/5-FU/C.
The transition of the coordinating end of the ligand from protonated to a deprotonated state in this pH range is responsible for the observed release pattern with different pH. It is safe to conclude that the folic acid-loaded dual MOF with the dual drug delivery system is more responsive to acidic pH than basic pH. To control drug release, synthesis techniques are also crucial and need to be taken into specific consideration for every drug. Drugs with a quick metabolism and elimination from the body usually benefit from prolonged sustained release. By stabilizing the drug’s plasma concentrations consistently, release may decline, resulting in greater drug dosages. The main drug release mechanisms utilized for sustained release are solvent-mediated release, diffusion process, and degradation [, ]. The release characteristics of the two anticancer agents can be compared and partially understood by comparing the structures of the anticancer agents. In this study, C drug release is much lower than 5-FU. A seven-membered carbon chain separates the two aryl rings of the large molecule C. Even with the carbonyl groups, the structure is sparingly soluble in water [, ]. The high loadings (~ 50% or more) observed in various studies for C loading in MOF structures due to the hydrophobic nature of the molecule. The theoretical modeling survey has shed light on the sights where C loads in MOF structures. C loading in MOF structures begins when C successively occupies hydrophobic areas (between ligand structures). In these phases and areas, there was no evidence of clustering. Once all hydrophobic regions have been loaded, the drug penetrates hydrophilic areas in the MOF volume at higher quantities. In these hydrophilic areas, C clusters upon further loading.
Interestingly, despite the drug’s hydrophobic nature, the study found that it had fast-release properties, releasing up to 100% of its contents in less than an hour. The solubilization of C by the ligands following the MOF complex’s dissolution may account for C releasing properties. While the imidazole ligands employed in this investigation were more difficult to build inclusion structures with C [].
C release from the M-MOF74 family of MOFs containing various metal ions was also used to study C’s interaction with the metal centers in the MOF structure. Research revealed that the amount of loading is independent of the type of metal and that there is less metal interaction with C. In the meantime, it appeared that the drug release was contingent upon the kind of metal ion, or more specifically, its solubility; in terms of the delivery system’s release kinetics are determined by the degradation of the MOF structure [].
Studies of C loading in zirconium 1,4-dicarboxybenzene (UiO66) MOF structure, a MOF structure with similar pore volume (0.41 m3/g) showing high loading (~ 49%) depicted release kinetics plateauing by 3 h []In contrast, the dual MOF structure in the current study showed sustained release for 10 h, a threefold increase.
On the other hand, 5-FU is a small molecule compared to C. 5-FU is highly soluble in essential solutions (50 mg/mL) compared to the sparingly soluble C []. As observed, the drug molecules release occurs through the MOF structure’s disintegration. The crucial nature of imidazole from the disintegration can help in the solubility of 5-FU. On the other hand, the more hydrophobic nature and larger size of C ensures that the anti-cancer agent is located in deeper hydrophobic pockets in the MOF structure compared to 5-FU, which can quickly enter into dipolar interactions like hydrogen bonding due to its smaller size. The ease of access to the interface and the solubility led to the observed kinetics in present drug release findings. ## Conclusion
A gold nanoparticle encapsulated dual MOF architecture functionalized with targeting ligand for combination drug delivery of 5FU and C drugs for a pH-responsive drug delivery system has been reported. A significant affinity for anticancer agent loading is demonstrated by the ZIF8/ZIF67 dual MOF composite, which allows for the simultaneous loading of 40% of 5-FU and 60% of C for the synthesized drug delivery system. The pH responsiveness of the delivery system is determined by the location of the ligand ionization equilibrium, 2-methylimidazole. As a result, the systems respond more strongly to an acidic pH, and the cumulative release rises in the following order: pH 5 > pH 6 > pH 7.4. The observed higher drug release behaviors of 5FU compared to C is due to its more hydrophobic and larger size; whereas C is situated in deeper hydrophobic pockets within the MOF structure. At the same time, 5-FU, being smaller, can rapidly engage in dipolar interactions such as hydrogen bonding. The cancer-targeting functionalization, enhanced release at lower pH, and nanoparticle modification can make this composite suitable for cancer management, and this system may act as an anticancer therapeutic agent after a few essential studies.
