# Sustainable Synthesis of Trimetallic Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF Nanorods from Recycled PET Waste for Ultrafast Ciprofloxacin Adsorption: Performance and Structure–Activity Relationship
## Abstract
AbstractDespite continuous efforts to mitigate antibiotic resistance, the persistent accumulation of ciprofloxacin (CIP) in aquatic systems remains a growing environmental concern, underscoring the need for effective and sustainable removal strategies. In this study, a novel trimetallic metal–organic framework nanomaterial (Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF) was successfully synthesized via a one-step solvothermal approach, utilizing a terephthalic acid linker sustainably derived from postconsumer PET waste. The synthesized material was characterized by XRD, FT-IR, SEM, BET, and TGA analyses. The Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF exhibited a rapid adsorption response, achieving 90% removal of CIP within only 5 min under nonoptimized conditions. Under optimized conditions (10 mg L–1 CIP concentration, 25 mg adsorbent dose, pH 5, contact time 20 min), the removal efficiency reached 97.6%, with a maximum adsorption capacity of 224.8 mg g–1. The adsorption kinetics followed the pseudo-second-order model, and the equilibrium data were best fitted to the Langmuir isotherm, indicating monolayer adsorption driven by complex physicochemical interactions. The superior performance was attributed to a multifaceted synergistic effect, characterized by a significant expansion of the specific surface area (1122 m2 g–1) and refined pore architecture, coupled with an optimized surface charge (pHpzc = 4.6) that enhances electrostatic attraction. Furthermore, the Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF demonstrated outstanding hydrolytic stability and reusability, retaining 90.8% efficiency over five consecutive cycles with negligible metal leaching and retained crystallinity, highlighting its strong potential for the sustainable treatment of pharmaceutical wastewater.
## Introduction
In recent decades, pollution of aquatic ecosystems has emerged as one of the most pressing global environmental challenges, driven by industrial expansion and rapid population growth. Among the emerging pollutants of growing concern are pharmaceutical residues, particularly antibiotics, due to their intensive use in human and veterinary medicine and in plant communities, − The unregulated discharge of these life-saving compounds into the environment, particularly in developing countries, leads to their accumulation in various water bodies, including wastewater, rivers, lakes, and even drinking water, posing a serious threat to public health and the environment, −
Ciprofloxacin (CIP), a second-generation broad-spectrum antibiotic of the fluoroquinolone family, is one of the most important and widespread pharmaceutical pollutants in wastewater. , Ciprofloxacin is characterized by its stable chemical structure and limited biodegradability, making it a persistent pollutant in the environment. , The presence of these compounds, even in minute concentrations, directly contributes to the exacerbation of antimicrobial resistance (AMR), which the World Health Organization considers an urgent global health crisis, in addition to their potential negative effects on aquatic organisms. ,
To address this challenge, several water treatment technologies have been explored, such as advanced oxidation processes, , membrane filtration, photodegradation, , and adsorption. , However, these methods are often expensive, operationally complex, or may lead to the formation of harmful byproducts. Among these technologies, adsorption stands out as a promising and preferred solution due to its simplicity, high cost-effectiveness, and ability to remove contaminants without producing toxic compounds. The success of this process depends mainly on the properties of the adsorbent used, which has prompted researchers to seek to develop new adsorbents with high efficiency, stability, selectivity, and large surface area.
Several adsorbents have been reported for removing fluoroquinolones from aqueous solutions, for example, graphene oxide, activated carbon, and multiwalled carbon nanotubes. In this context, metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) have emerged as exceptionally promising porous crystalline materials. These materials consist of metal ions or clusters (metal nodes) linked by coordinated organic ligands, forming three-dimensional networks with a specific crystalline structure. MOFs have unique properties, including enormous specific surface area, large pore volume, high tunable porosity, good chemical and thermal stability, and the ability to easily modify their chemical functions. These unique features have made metal–organic frameworks an ideal candidate for many applications, such as gas storage, , catalysis, drug delivery, sensing, and particularly in the field of water purification.
Recently, studies have shown that the use of multivariate or bimetallic/trimetallic MOFs can significantly enhance their effectiveness. − The integration of more than one type of metal ion into a single structure creates a synergistic effect between the different metal centers, improving the electronic and adsorption properties of the material and increasing the number of active sites available for interaction, −
In this work, with the aim of developing a new and improved material, we synthesized a ternary metal–organic framework (Al/Mg/Fe) using terephthalic acid extracted from PET plastic waste as an organic linker, via a thermal solvent method. This material was designed to combine the unique properties of the three metals in order to enhance its ability to adsorb ciprofloxacin from aqueous solutions. The prepared material was comprehensively characterized, and its efficiency in removing ciprofloxacin was studied under different conditions of contact time, pH, adsorbent dosage, and temperature. The adsorption kinetics, isotherm models, and thermodynamics were analyzed to gain a deeper understanding of the reaction mechanism, and the reusability of the material was evaluated. This research aims to provide a new, effective, and sustainable adsorbent for treating antibiotic-contaminated water.
## Experimental Section
## Materials
Aluminum sulfate hexadecahydrate (Al2(SO4)3·16H2O), magnesium sulfate heptahydrate (MgSO4·7H2O), ferric chloride hexahydrate (FeCl3·6H2O), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), sodium hydroxide (NaOH), hydrochloric acid (HCl), ethanol (C2H5OH), sodium chloride (NaCl), and antibiotic ciprofloxacin (CIP) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. All chemicals were of analytical grade and used as received without further purification. Deionized water was used throughout the experiments. Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid (H2BDC; 99.5% yield, 98.3% purity from postconsumer PET waste) was used as the organic linker.
## Synthesis of MOF Materials
The trimetallic Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF was synthesized via a facile one-step solvothermal method. In a typical synthesis, the organic linker, terephthalic acid (H2BDC, 0.34 g, 2.046 mmol), was first dissolved in 29 mL of DMF. This linker was recovered from postconsumer PET waste using our previously published, fully characterized procedure, ensuring high purity and reproducibility suitable for reproducible MOF synthesis. Separately, a mixed-metal precursor solution was prepared by dissolving FeCl3·6H2O (0.15 g, 0.555 mmol), Al2(SO4)3·16H2O (0.3 g, 0.476 mmol), and MgSO4·7H2O (0.22 g, 0.893 mmol) in 9 mL of deionized water. The aqueous metal solution was then added dropwise to the H2BDC/DMF solution under vigorous stirring. After 20 min, the final mixture was transferred to a 50 mL Teflon-lined autoclave and heated at 120 °C for 24 h. The resulting solid product was recovered by filtration, washed sequentially with DMF and ethanol, and finally dried in an oven at 80 °C for 12 h. For comparative purposes, the monometallic Al-BDC and bimetallic Al/Mg-BDC analogues were synthesized under identical conditions, using only the corresponding metal precursors while maintaining the same overall ligand-to-total-metal molar ratio.
## Characterization
The crystallinity and phase purity of the synthesized MOFs were characterized using a PHILIPS PW1730 X-ray diffractometer (XRD) with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å). FT-IR analysis was conducted on a PerkinElmer TWO spectrometer (USA) to identify key functional groups. The surface morphology and elemental composition were explored using a MIRA3 TESCAN scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipped with an energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) detector, operating at an accelerating voltage of 20.0 kV. Mass loss and thermal stability were performed by Thermal Gravimetric Analysis (TGA) with instrument TG 50 (Mettler Toledo, Switzerland) under a nitrogen flow, with a heating rate of 10 °C/min from 30 to 950 °C. Surface area and porosity analysis was performed on a BELSORP-mini analyzer (Japan) via nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherms at 77 K, following sample degassing at 120 °C for 5 h. The residual concentration of the adsorbed ciprofloxacin was determined using a JASCO V-630 UV–vis spectrophotometer (Japan) at a wavelength of 276 nm, using deionized water as a reference.
## Adsorption Experiments
The adsorptive removal of ciprofloxacin (CIP) from aqueous solutions was investigated in a series of batch experiments. The influence of key parameters including contact time (5–60 min), adsorbent dosage (5–30 mg), initial solution pH (2–10), and initial CIP concentration (10–300 mg L–1) was systematically studied. All experiments were conducted at room temperature (25 ± 2 °C) under dark conditions, with agitation provided by a magnetic stirrer (300 rpm). The initial pH of the solutions was adjusted as required using 0.1 M HCl or 0.1 M NaOH.
Following each experiment, the adsorbent was separated from the solution by centrifugation (8000 rpm, 10 min). The residual CIP concentration in the supernatant was then determined by UV–vis spectrophotometry at λmax = 276 nm. The experimental data were used to evaluate the adsorption kinetics, equilibrium isotherms, and thermodynamic properties of the process. All experiments were performed in triplicate, and the average values are reported. ## Determination of Point of Zero Charge (pHpzc)
The point of zero charge (pHpzc) of the trimetallic Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF was determined using the pH drift method. The pHpzc represents the pH at which the adsorbent’s surface has a net neutral charge. At pH values below the pHpzc, the surface becomes protonated and exhibits a net positive charge, while at pH values above the pHpzc, it becomes deprotonated, resulting in a net negative charge. This parameter is crucial for understanding the electrostatic interactions between the adsorbent and the adsorbate. In this procedure, a series of 25 mL solutions of 0.1 M NaCl were prepared, and their initial pH (pHi) was adjusted to values ranging from 2 to 10 using 0.1 M HCl or 0.1 M NaOH. To each solution, 20 mg of the adsorbent was added. The suspensions were then agitated for 24 h at room temperature to allow the system to reach equilibrium. After agitation, the final pH (pHf) of each suspension was measured. The pHpzc was determined from the intersection point of the pHf versus pHi curve with the line where pHf = pHi.
## Results and Discussion
## Characterization Results
## XRD Analysis
The powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) patterns of the synthesized materials are presented in Figure . The monometallic Al-BDC exhibits characteristic reflections at 2θ = 9.2°, 15.4°, 17.8°, 25.4°, and 30.1°, corresponding to the (101), (011), (202), (312), and (313) planes, respectively, which are in excellent agreement with the standard MIL-53­(Al) pattern (CCDC 220475). Upon the simultaneous incorporation of Mg2+ and Fe3+, the multimetallic MOFs maintain the characteristic MIL-53 topology, as evidenced by the preservation of the primary diffraction profile. However, a systematic shift of the main reflection from 9.2° to 9.8° is observed in the Al/Mg/Fe-BDC sample. While the substitution of Al3+ (0.535 Å) with the larger Mg2+ (0.72 Å) might intuitively suggest lattice expansion, the observed shift toward higher 2θ angles reflects a lattice contraction associated with the stabilization of the narrow-pore (np) phase over the large-pore (lp) phase at room temperature; this structural response is driven by modified host–guest interactions and enhanced internal hydrogen bonding within the inorganic chains. − Furthermore, the aliovalent substitution of divalent Mg2+ into the trivalent Al3+/Fe3+ lattice is charge-compensated by the protonation of the bridging hydroxyl groups, transforming the μ2–OH units into μ2–OH2 (coordinated water) species. This mechanism effectively neutralizes the excess negative charge generated by the lower-valent magnesium ions, ensuring framework stability as supported by recent studies on aliovalent MOF solid solutions. , The absence of additional peaks for MgO or Fe-oxides confirms the high phase purity and the successful integration of the multiple metal ions into a homogeneous crystalline framework. The collective crystallographic evidence is therefore consistent with the predominance of the narrow-pore configuration in the trimetallic framework under ambient conditions, in line with the breathing behavior widely reported for MIL-53-type materials upon metal substitution, wherein the incorporation of foreign metal cations into the inorganic chains systematically alters the lp/np phase equilibrium as evidenced by uniform shifts in PXRD reflection positions , and further supported by recent analysis of aliovalent MOF solid solutions.
## FTIR Analysis
Fourier-Transform Infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy was employed to confirm the successful coordination of the terephthalic acid (BDC) linker to the metal centers. The FT-IR spectra of the free BDC linker and the synthesized MOFs are presented in Figure . The spectrum of the free BDC linker exhibits a characteristic sharp peak at approximately 1685 cm–1, corresponding to the CO stretching vibration of the protonated carboxyl group. Upon formation of the MOFs, this peak disappears and is replaced by two new, distinct bands at approximately 1590 cm–1 and 1411 cm–1. These bands are assigned to the asymmetric (νas­(COO–)) and symmetric (νs­(COO–)) stretching vibrations of the deprotonated carboxylate group, respectively. , This spectral shift provides definitive evidence that the BDC linker has successfully coordinated to the metal centers. Furthermore, the appearance of new bands in the low-frequency region (480–600 cm–1) for the MOF samples can be attributed to the formation of metal–oxygen (M–O) bonds, further confirming successful coordination. , Additionally, all MOF spectra display a prominent broad absorption band centered around 3440 cm–1. This band can be ascribed to the O–H stretching vibrations of crystalline water molecules (both coordinated and adsorbed) within the porous MOF structure. The overall similarity of the fingerprint regions (below 1600 cm–1) across all synthesized materials confirms that they share the same fundamental framework, differing primarily in their metallic composition.

As illustrated in (Figure a–d), the SEM micrographs reveal that the material is composed of compact aggregates consisting of closely packed nanorod-like particles. Diameter measurements of 91 individual nanorods were performed using ImageJ software, yielding a mean diameter of 30.74 ± 9.36 nm, confirming the formation of a well-defined 1D nanostructure. The corresponding size distribution histogram (Figure e) further illustrates the relatively narrow size distribution of the nanorods, consistent with a controlled synthesis process. This rod-based morphology is expected to enhance the accessible surface area and facilitate efficient contact with guest species, which is consistent with the high porosity and adsorption performance discussed in subsequent sections. ## TGA Analysis
The TGA curve of the synthesized Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF (Figure ) reveals a single-step decomposition profile with an onset temperature of 436.69 °C, indicating substantial thermal stability of the framework structure. The decomposition proceeds rapidly with an inflection point at 438.67 °C and a midpoint at 441.39 °C, resulting in a total mass loss of 64.49%. The residual mass of 35.50% at elevated temperatures is attributed to the formation of thermally stable metal oxides. The absence of significant mass loss below 400 °C suggests minimal solvent content and robust framework integrity prior to thermal decomposition. These findings confirm the high thermal stability of the synthesized material, rendering it suitable for high-temperature applications.
## Effects of Different Parameters on the Adsorption of CIP
## Effect of Contact Time
To determine the equilibrium time and understand the kinetics of the adsorption process, the effect of contact time on ciprofloxacin (CIP) removal by the trimetallic Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF was investigated. The experiments were conducted using 20 mg of the adsorbent dispersed in 25 mL of a 10 mg L–1 CIP solution at a neutral pH of 7. The results, depicted in Figure , reveal a remarkably rapid adsorption process. A substantial removal efficiency of 90% was achieved within the first 5 min, corresponding to an adsorption capacity (q t ) of 11.25 mg g–1. This initial fast uptake phase is characteristic of processes where a high number of readily accessible active sites are available on the adsorbent surface. The system then approached equilibrium, reaching a removal efficiency of 93.9% (11.74 mg g–1) at the 20 min mark. Beyond this point, the adsorption rate slowed considerably, with only a marginal increase to a maximum of 94.3% (11.79 mg g–1) observed at 60 min. This trend confirms that equilibrium was effectively established around 20 min. The ability of the Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF to achieve such rapid and high-efficiency removal demonstrates the high affinity and excellent mass transport properties of the synthesized framework. Therefore, a contact time of 20 min was selected as the optimal duration for all subsequent batch experiments.
## Effect of Adsorbent Dosage
The effect of the Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF dosage on ciprofloxacin (CIP) removal was studied by varying the adsorbent dosage from 0.2 to 1.2 g L–1 (equivalent to 5 to 30 mg in a 25 mL solution). The experiments were conducted using an initial CIP concentration of 10 mg L–1 at pH 7 for 20 min. As shown in Figure , increasing the dosage resulted in a higher removal efficiency, which rose from 87.5% to 94.8%. This is attributed to the greater availability of active sites at higher dosages. Conversely, the adsorption capacity (q e) decreased from 43.8 to 7.9 mg g–1, a result of a lower adsorbate-to-adsorbent ratio. Since the removal efficiency showed only a marginal increase beyond 1.0 g L–1 (25 mg), this dosage was selected as the optimal value for subsequent experiments, as it provides a balance between high removal and efficient material use.
## Effect of pH
The experimental results presented in Figure a demonstrate the pronounced effect of solution pH on the removal efficiency of ciprofloxacin (CIP) by the Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF adsorbent. These experiments were performed under fixed conditions, namely an initial CIP concentration of 10 ppm, a contact time of 20 min, and an adsorbent dose of 25 mg. Removal efficiency peaked at pH 5 with a removal rate of 97.6%, and then gradually decreased to 74.8% at pH 10. This behavior can be rationalized by considering the interplay between the ionization state of CIP and the surface charge of the adsorbent. Ciprofloxacin exhibits two pK a values, pK a1 = 6.09 and pK a2 = 8.62, while the point of zero charge (pHpzc) of the adsorbent is 4.6 (Figure b). At pH 2 and 4 (pH < pHpzc), the adsorbent surface is positively charged, which leads to electrostatic repulsion with cationic CIP (predominant at pH < pK a1), resulting in a lower removal efficiency. At the optimal pH of 5 (slightly above the pHpzc), the adsorbent surface becomes negatively charged, whereas CIP mainly exists in its cationic and partially zwitterionic forms. This charge contrast markedly promotes electrostatic attraction. In addition, nonelectrostatic interactions, such as hydrogen bonding and π–π stacking between the aromatic rings of CIP and the BDC linkers of the MOF, further enhance the removal performance. As the pH increases from 6 to 10, CIP progressively shifts from its zwitterionic to its anionic form (at pH > pK a2). Because the adsorbent surface remains negatively charged in this pH range, the growing electrostatic repulsion between anionic CIP and the negatively charged surface becomes a key factor, accounting for the significant decrease in removal efficiency observed at pH 8 and 10.
## Effect of Initial CIP Concentration
The effect of the initial ciprofloxacin (CIP) concentration (C 0) on both removal efficiency and equilibrium adsorption capacity (q e) by the Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF was investigated at fixed conditions of 25 mg adsorbent dose, 20 min contact time, and pH 5. As presented in Figure , a clear inverse relationship was observed between C 0 and removal efficiency, which decreased from 97.6% to 71.7% as the concentration increased from 10 to 300 mg L–1. This decline is expected, as the fixed adsorbent dosage provides a finite number of active sites that become saturated at higher concentrations, resulting in lower percentage removal of CIP molecules from solution. In contrast, the adsorption capacity (q e) increased significantly with rising C 0, from 9.76 to 215.2 mg g–1. This positive trend is attributed to the enhanced concentration gradient at higher initial concentrations, which provides a strong driving force that overcomes mass transfer resistances and promotes diffusion of CIP molecules to the adsorbent surface.

## Adsorption Thermodynamics
To investigate the thermodynamic feasibility and energetic nature of the adsorption process, the effect of temperature on the removal of ciprofloxacin (CIP) by Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF was evaluated at 25, 35, 45, and 55 °C. This inverse relationship suggests that the adsorption process is inherently exothermic, likely due to the progressive weakening of interactions between CIP molecules and the active sites of the MOF at elevated temperatures.

Overall, these findings demonstrate that the adsorption of CIP onto the Al/Mg/Fe-BDC MOF is a spontaneous and exothermic process, governed by surface interactions with a tendency toward increased structural ordering at the interface.

## Comparative Adsorption Performance and Structure–Activity Relationship
The influence of multimetal integration on CIP removal was rigorously assessed by comparing Al-BDC, Al/Mg-BDC, and Al/Mg/Fe-BDC under identical experimental conditions (C 0 = 10 mg L–1, dose = 25 mg, pH 5.0). As shown in Figure , the trimetallic Al/Mg/Fe-BDC displayed a markedly enhanced adsorption profile, reaching a removal efficiency above 97% within 20 min, whereas Al-BDC and Al/Mg-BDC achieved approximately 79% and 85%, respectively, over the same period. Complementing this fast kinetic performance, equilibrium adsorption isotherms revealed that the trimetallic framework attained a Langmuir maximum adsorption capacity of 224.8 mg g–1, significantly outperforming its mono- and bimetallic counterparts (178.5 and 198.9 mg g–1, respectively). This progressive enhancement is attributed to the complementary modulation of textural and surface-chemical properties across the three frameworks, as quantified in Table . From a textural standpoint, BET surface area increases progressively from 752.2 (Al-BDC) to 1020.0 (Al/Mg-BDC) to 1122.0 m2 g–1 (Al/Mg/Fe-BDC). Notably, the surface-area-normalized adsorption capacity (q/BET) shows a slight decrease from 0.237 (Al-BDC) to 0.195 mg m–2 (Al/Mg-BDC), then partially recovers to 0.200 mg m–2 in the trimetallic framework. This trend suggests that while the intrinsic efficiency per unit surface area does not show a progressive increase across the series, the observed enhancement in overall adsorption capacity is primarily driven by the significantly larger accessible surface area and the concurrent refinement of the average pore radius from 12.2 to 11.6 to 10.0 Å. The 10.0 Å channel diameter of Al/Mg/Fe-BDC is particularly well-matched to the molecular dimensions of CIP (width ≈ 7–8 Å), promoting enhanced host–guest confinement and stronger van der Waals interactions within the pore channels. The decrease in total pore volume from 0.590 (Al/Mg-BDC) to 0.561 cm3 g–1 (Al/Mg/Fe-BDC), despite a concurrent BET increase, further corroborates the formation of a larger number of narrower pores upon Fe3+ incorporation, consistent with progressive narrow-pore (np) phase stabilization as evidenced by PXRD. From a surface-chemical standpoint, the pHpzc values of the three frameworks are 4.8, 4.9, and 4.6, respectively (Figure b). The slight increase in pHpzc upon Mg2+ incorporation reflects the reduced Lewis acidity of the surface due to the aliovalent nature of Mg2+, while the subsequent decrease to 4.6 upon Fe3+ addition reflects the stronger Lewis acidity of Fe3+ centers and their dominance in modulating surface charge. At the operating pH of 5.0, the trimetallic framework carries the largest negative surface charge (ΔpH = pH – pHpzc = +0.4), compared to +0.1 for Al/Mg-BDC and +0.2 for Al-BDC, thereby facilitating the strongest electrostatic attraction toward the positively charged ciprofloxacin species under the applied conditions. Collectively, the superior adsorption performance of the trimetallic framework reflects the concurrent contribution of three independently quantifiable factors: the highest accessible surface area (1122.0 m2 g–1), the most favorable pore geometry for CIP confinement (r = 10.0 Å), and the greatest electrostatic driving force at pH 5.0 (lowest pHpzc = 4.6). The progressive and consistent improvement across the mono–bi–trimetallic series demonstrates that sequential metal incorporation rationally tunes the structure–activity relationship of the BDC-based framework toward optimized antibiotic adsorption.
## Effect of Coexisting Ions
The influence of coexisting ions on the adsorption performance was investigated under the optimized experimental conditions, including the previously determined optimum pH of 5, at which the highest removal efficiency of Ciprofloxacin was achieved. Common inorganic cations (Na+, Ca2+, and Mg2+) were introduced into the solution using their corresponding chloride salts. As presented in Figure , the adsorption capacity slightly decreased from 215.2 mg g–1 in the absence of salts to 213.8, 211, and 209.3 mg g–1 in the presence of Na+, Ca2+, and Mg2+, respectively.
This marginal reduction can be mainly attributed to the increase in ionic strength and the competitive interactions between dissolved cations and ciprofloxacin molecules for the available active sites. The presence of electrolytes leads to compression of the electrical double layer surrounding the adsorbent, thereby weakening electrostatic interactions. In addition, cations may interact with functional groups of ciprofloxacin or occupy negatively charged sites on the MOF surface, which partially hinders adsorption. The more pronounced inhibitory effect observed for divalent cations (Ca2+ and Mg2+) compared to monovalent Na+ is consistent with their higher charge density and stronger competitive affinity toward adsorption sites. It should be noted that chloride ions (Cl–), introduced via the corresponding salts, are generally considered weakly interacting and noncomplexing species under the studied conditions; therefore, the observed effects are predominantly governed by cationic competition and ionic strength rather than specific anion–adsorbent interactions. Furthermore, at pH 5, ciprofloxacin exists predominantly in a protonated or zwitterionic form, and thus the adsorption mechanism is not governed solely by electrostatic attraction. Instead, additional interactions such as π–π interactions between the aromatic rings, hydrogen bonding, and pore-filling within the porous framework play a significant role. As a result, the adsorbent retained more than 97% of its initial adsorption capacity even in the presence of competing ions, demonstrating strong resistance to ionic interference and confirming its suitability for application in complex aqueous environments..
## Reusability and Stability Assessment
Reusability is a critical parameter for evaluating the economic feasibility and practical applicability of adsorbent materials in large-scale wastewater treatment. The regeneration performance of Al/Mg/Fe-BDC was evaluated over five consecutive adsorption–desorption cycles. After each cycle, the spent adsorbent was separated, washed thoroughly with an ethanol/water mixture, and dried before reuse. As illustrated in Figure , the adsorption efficiency exhibited a gradual decline from 97.6% in the first cycle to 90.8% after the fifth cycle, representing a total decrease of approximately 6.8%, which reflects the practical robustness of the material under repeated use.
To investigate the structural basis of this performance decline, PXRD analysis was performed after the fifth cycle (Figure ). The characteristic diffraction peaks of the MIL-53-based framework remain well-preserved, confirming the retention of crystalline topology after multiple adsorption–desorption cycles. BET surface area analysis of the postcycling material yielded a value of 621.7 m2 g–1, compared to 1122.0 m2 g–1 for the fresh adsorbent, representing a 44.6% reduction in accessible surface area. Given the intact PXRD patterns, this moderate porosity loss is attributed to partial pore blockage by residual CIP molecules that were not completely desorbed during regeneration, rather than structural collapse or framework degradation. The preserved crystallinity alongside the partial porosity reduction is consistent with the observed 6.8% decline in removal efficiency, establishing a direct structure-performance correlation.
Chemical stability was further corroborated by FT-IR analysis (Figure ), which revealed a largely unchanged spectral profile before and after reuse. Minor shifts in the asymmetric and symmetric stretching vibrations of the carboxylate bands (from 1599.5 and 1416 cm–1 to 1589 and 1411 cm–1, respectively), along with a reduction in the intensity of the −OH band (∼3435 cm–1), confirm the involvement of surface functional groups in CIP binding likely through hydrogen bonding and surface complexation mechanisms, consistent with previous reports on antibiotic adsorption by MOF-based materials, , without compromising the chemical integrity of the framework.
The environmental safety of the process was validated by AAS analysis of the postadsorption supernatant. Metal concentrations in the treated solution were: Al = 0.8 mg L–1, Fe = 0.4 mg L–1, and Mg = 3.0 mg L–1. All values are well within the permissible limits for wastewater discharge, confirming the hydrolytic stability of the trimetallic framework under the applied conditions. Collectively, the preserved crystallinity, moderate and explicable porosity reduction, unchanged chemical structure, and acceptable metal leaching levels demonstrate that Al/Mg/Fe-BDC is a structurally robust and environmentally safe adsorbent suitable for repeated use in water treatment applications.
## Adsorption Capacity Comparison
As shown in Table , the adsorption capacities and equilibrium times of the synthesized Al–BDC, Al/Mg–BDC, and Al/Mg/Fe–BDC MOFs are compared with those of various previously reported adsorbents. The Al/Mg/Fe–BDC MOF exhibited a notably high adsorption capacity (224.8 mg g–1) and an exceptionally short equilibrium time of 0.33 h. This performance surpasses that of many conventional adsorbents such as activated carbon (231 mg g–1, 72 h) and graphene oxide (379 mg g–1, 48 h). Notably, while high-capacity adsorbents like graphene oxide and ZIF-8-derived carbon require prolonged contact times of 48 and 18 h to reach equilibrium, these MOFs achieved stability within only 0.33 h (20 min), thereby highlighting their ultrafast adsorption kinetics. In comparison with other MOF-based adsorbents, such as MOF-5 (98.2 mg g–1, 90 h) and MIL-53­(Fe–Cu) (190.4 mg g–1, 2.7 h), the ternary Al/Mg/Fe–BDC structure demonstrates an excellent balance between adsorption capacity and kinetic efficiency. The enhanced performance can be attributed to the synergistic effect among the incorporated metal ions, which increases the density of active sites, improves pore accessibility, and facilitates faster adsorbate diffusion through the MOF framework. These findings highlight the potential of multimetal BDC-based MOFs as highly efficient and kinetically favorable adsorbents for environmental remediation applications.

## Conclusion
In this study, a novel trimetallic Al/Mg/Fe–BDC MOF with a well-defined one-dimensional nanorod morphology and a high surface area of approximately 1122 m2 g–1 was successfully developed and evaluated as an efficient adsorbent for ciprofloxacin (CIP) removal from aqueous media. The trimetallic framework exhibited a markedly enhanced adsorption performance compared with its mono- and bimetallic analogues (Al–BDC and Al/Mg–BDC), achieving a maximum adsorption capacity of 224.8 mg g–1 and a removal efficiency above 97% within 20 min under optimized conditions, while Al–BDC and Al/Mg–BDC reached only 79% and 85%, respectively. This superior behavior is attributed to a multifaceted synergistic effect involving textural optimization and surface-charge modulation. Specifically, the strategic integration of Al, Mg, and Fe significantly expanded the accessible surface area and refined the pore architecture (average pore radius of 10.0 Å), optimizing host–guest confinement, while simultaneously lowering the pHpzc to 4.6 to strengthen electrostatic attraction toward cationic CIP species. The adsorption process followed a pseudo-second-order kinetic model and was well described by the Langmuir isotherm, indicating predominantly monolayer adsorption. Thermodynamic analysis (ΔG° < 0, ΔH° = −34.49 kJ mol–1, ΔS° < 0) confirmed that CIP adsorption is spontaneous and exothermic, involving strong physicochemical interactions such as electrostatic attraction, hydrogen bonding, π–π stacking, and surface complexation. Postcycling assessments confirmed the retention of crystallinity and chemical integrity via PXRD and FT-IR, while reusability over five cycles showed a retention of 90.8% efficiency, with the minor decline attributed to partial pore blockage. Overall, the combination of high capacity, ultrafast kinetics, and robust stability is complemented by a cost-effective synthesis utilizing abundant PET waste. This approach lowers raw material costs and promotes a circular economy, positioning the Al/Mg/Fe–BDC MOF as a scalable and practically viable candidate for large-scale pharmaceutical wastewater remediation.
